## Business Environment
China is a large, highly regulated market with major commercial hubs in Beijing, Shanghai and other cities. Users may be focused on market entry, representative presence, finance, technology, manufacturing, trading, professional services or China-facing regional operations.

## Company Incorporation Overview
Foreign investment and company establishment are governed by national and local rules. Common structures include foreign-invested enterprises, branches or representative offices, depending on business activity. Registration, licensing, negative-list restrictions, tax, labour and local administrative requirements must be checked with current official sources and advisers.

## Language
Mandarin Chinese is the primary business and official language. English may be used in international business settings, but official filings and many contracts require Chinese-language documentation.

## Currency
Chinese yuan/renminbi (CNY/RMB).

## Public Holidays
Public holidays commonly include New Year's Day, Spring Festival, Qingming Festival, Labour Day, Dragon Boat Festival, Mid-Autumn Festival and National Day. China may announce adjusted working days and holiday bridge days annually, so scheduling must check the State Council holiday notice for the relevant year.

## Payment Methods
Common business payment methods include bank transfer, corporate card where accepted, invoice-based settlement and local digital payment rails in some contexts. Fapiao, tax documentation, foreign exchange and cross-border settlement requirements may apply and must be handled through approved finance guidance.

## Business Culture
Business culture can be relationship-driven, formal and process-oriented. Users often expect local-language support, precise building/address details, fast follow-up, and clear distinction between what is available now and what requires government or landlord approval.

## Compliance Considerations
Verify foreign investment restrictions, business scope, licensing, tax/fapiao requirements, labour rules, data/cybersecurity requirements, foreign exchange controls, address registration rules and local authority requirements. Do not provide legal or tax advice.

## Visa Overview
Foreign nationals working in China generally require appropriate work authorisation and residence permits. Business visits, employment and investor/representative roles are different and must be checked against current official requirements.
