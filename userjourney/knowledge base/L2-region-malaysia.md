## Business Environment
Malaysia is a Southeast Asian business base with strong activity in Kuala Lumpur, especially around finance, energy, technology, professional services, trading and regional support operations. KLCC and KL Sentral are common corporate-location anchors for multinational and local companies.

## Company Incorporation Overview
Company registration is handled by the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM). Foreign-owned companies and foreign branches may be possible, but structure, licensing, director, shareholding, tax and sector-specific rules must be checked with SSM and professional advisers.

## Language
Malay is the national language. English is widely used in business, especially in Kuala Lumpur and multinational-company settings. Mandarin, Tamil and other languages may also be relevant depending on customer segment and location.

## Currency
Malaysian ringgit (MYR).

## Public Holidays
Malaysia has national and state-level public holidays. Common holidays include New Year's Day in many states, Chinese New Year, Hari Raya Aidilfitri, Labour Day, Wesak Day, Hari Raya Haji, National Day, Malaysia Day, Deepavali and Christmas. Some holidays differ by state or are announced annually, so centre operations and booking logic should check the official calendar for Kuala Lumpur and the relevant year.

## Payment Methods
Common business payment methods include bank transfer, corporate card, DuitNow/FPX, cheque in some B2B contexts and invoice settlement. Exact accepted methods, SST/tax handling, deposits and billing cycles must come from CEO SUITE commercial rules or live billing data.

## Business Culture
Business culture is relationship-oriented but still formal for corporate transactions. Users may expect clear written proposals, polite follow-up, attention to seniority and practical details such as parking, building access and proximity to transit.

## Compliance Considerations
Verify SSM registration, tax registration, sector licences, local authority permits, employment rules, data protection, foreign equity restrictions where relevant and expatriate pass eligibility. Do not infer legal, tax or licensing outcomes.

## Visa Overview
Foreign nationals working in Malaysia usually require the appropriate employment or expatriate pass, commonly handled through Malaysia immigration and the Expatriate Services Division where applicable. Business visitors and employees have different requirements.
